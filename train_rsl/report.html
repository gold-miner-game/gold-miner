Strategy and results (test)

Trained vs random

Each bar is the average over all test levels; the black line is the 95% confidence interval. If two intervals don't overlap, the difference is real and not luck.

Results per level

Left: how consistent it is. A tall bar on the right means it often clears the field. Right: catching the player ends the level, so catches bunched near 100% mean it takes the money first and catches the player at the end.

What it shoots at

Every shot, by what the hook came back with. A good miner has few empty shots and few rocks (rocks are heavy and almost worthless).

Collection order (its priorities)

For each level, the 1st mineral it brought back, the 2nd, and so on. If the trained AI's first grab is usually the Diamond or Gold, it has learned to go for the most valuable things first.

Timing and aiming

Random firing shoots about once a second wherever the swing happens to be. A trained miner waits for the swing to line up: longer waits, and most shots fired while the aim points at a mineral or the player.

Money over time

Average money as a level goes on (a level that ended early keeps its final amount). A steeper early rise means it grabs the valuable minerals quickly.

Where it aims

Each bar counts shots fired at that swing angle, colored by what came back. Peaks show the directions the AI has learned are worth shooting at.

Training curves

Is it learning?

Total reward the AI earned per level, averaged over each summary period. A rising curve means it is learning. The dashed line is what random firing scores in the test; the AI should end up well above it. A flat line from the start usually means the rewards are too sparse or the learning rate is too low.

Money

How much the hook brings back per level. 'Share' is money collected divided by all money on the field, so 100% means it cleared the field.

Catching the player

Catching the player gives a big reward but ends the level, so a smart miner collects money first and catches the player late. Watch the right chart: if it climbs, the AI is learning to wait.

Shooting

Empty shots cost time and a small penalty. A falling empty-shot rate means the AI fires when the swing points at something.

Training health

Entropy is how random the AI's choices still are: it should slowly go down as it becomes sure of its strategy (dropping to almost 0 very early means it stopped exploring). Value loss usually rises while rewards change and settles once learning levels off. Policy loss stays small and noisy; that is normal.
